Privacy Policy

Effective 22 November 2026

In short: you can use this site without an account, and nothing you mark leaves your browser. If you create an account, you choose a made-up username and a password. Your marks are encrypted in your browser before they're sent, so the server stores only scrambled data it can't read. There are no cookies, ads or trackers. Visits to the site are counted anonymously, without cookies.

Who runs this site

The PS Plus Rental Wall (the "site") is a free, personal, non-commercial project run by an individual (the "operator", "we" or "us"). You can reach the operator through the project's GitHub issues page. Please don't post your password or anything private there, because issues are public.

What this policy covers

This policy explains what information the site handles, where it goes and what choices you have. It applies to the site at wallofpsplus.com, its former address misterhundoah-code.github.io/Psplusorganizer (which now forwards to it), and to the small server (the "account service") that handles accounts and PlayStation Store lookups.

Using the site without an account

Everything you do on the site (your marks, ratings, notes, play log, games you add, filters and theme) is saved in your own browser's local storage. It isn't sent to us. Local storage is a feature of your browser that lets a page keep information on your device. Only this site can read what it saves there, and you can erase it at any time by clearing your browser's site data.

The site keeps these items in your browser:

ItemWhat it holds
psplus-marks, psplus-rate, psplus-lib, psplus-notes, psplus-dates, psplus-customYour marks, star ratings, library tags, notes, play-log dates and the games you've added
psplus-sorterYour filter and sort choices, and the country you picked
psplus-themeLight or dark mode, if you've picked one
psplus-welcomed, psplus-noticeWhether you've seen the welcome box and the storage notice, so they don't keep coming back
psplus-acctOnly while you're signed in: your account id, login token, encryption key and sync status, so you stay signed in. Signing out removes it.

The site moved from misterhundoah-code.github.io/Psplusorganizer to wallofpsplus.com. Browsers keep saved data separately for each address, so when you open the old address, the page packs your saved records (not your sign-in) into the part of the link after the #, which browsers don't send to any server, and opens the new address, which saves them in your browser there. Nothing is sent to us or stored anywhere else along the way.

Cookies and tracking

The site sets no cookies. It has no advertising, no social media buttons and no tracking pixels. The fonts and the game artwork are stored with the site itself, so your browser doesn't contact a font service or Sony to show them. Store links go to the PlayStation Store for the region you pick (or, by default, the one that matches your browser's language), and only when you follow one. The local storage items listed above are needed for the site to work the way you've asked it to, and none of them is used to track you.

Visit counts

To see how many people use the site and how they find it, each page loads a small script from Cloudflare Web Analytics. When a page loads, the script sends Cloudflare the page's address, the address of the page that linked to it, your browser and device type, your approximate country and how quickly the page loaded. Like any request, it also reaches Cloudflare with your IP address. The script sets no cookies, saves nothing in your browser and doesn't build a profile of you or follow you to other sites. We see only totals, such as visits per day, top countries and top referring sites, and never information about an individual visitor. The script doesn't see your marks, notes, username or password.

If you'd rather not be counted, a content or tracker blocker that blocks static.cloudflareinsights.com will stop the script, and the site works the same without it.

If you create an account

An account lets you sync your records between devices. We don't ask for your name, email address, phone number or any other contact details. Usernames containing @ are refused so that email addresses aren't used by mistake. Please pick a username that doesn't identify you.

When you sign up or sign in, your browser turns your username and password into three values: an account id, a login token and an encryption key. Your browser encrypts your records with the key before sending them. The key and your password never leave your browser. The account service stores only:

  • the account id, which is a one-way hash of your username;
  • a one-way hash of the login token;
  • your records in encrypted form, with a version number;
  • the dates the account was created and last updated; and
  • a count of recent failed sign-in attempts and, if there have been too many, the time a temporary lock ends.

The account service also keeps a count of how many accounts were created each day, with no details about any account, so it can cap sign-ups. Those counts are deleted after a week.

The account service doesn't store your username, your password, your IP address or any readable version of your records. Because we can't read your records or see your password, we can't recover a forgotten password or restore an account whose password is lost.

Community scores (optional)

If you have an account, you can tick Add my ratings to the community scores under Account. It's off unless you turn it on, and it's set separately on each device you use. While it's on, each time your records sync your browser also sends, without encryption:

  • for each game you've marked or rated, its id, your mark (playing, want to play, played, bounced off or not interested) and your star rating.

Your notes, dates, added games and settings are never sent. These votes are stored under a code made by hashing your account id, so the votes table doesn't contain account ids. The site only publishes totals for each game (the number of people, the average rating and how many played, bounced off, want to play or are playing it), and only for games with at least three people. Nobody else can see an individual person's votes.

Untick the box to withdraw your votes; they're deleted from the database straight away. Deleting your account deletes them too. The same recovery-copy period described below applies.

Adding a game from a PlayStation Store link

When you paste a PlayStation Store link and press Look up, the link is sent to the account service. The account service fetches that one public store page, reads the title, platforms, genres and release year, and sends them back to your browser. It doesn't save the link or the result. The game you add is then saved like the rest of your records: in your browser, and in encrypted form in your account if you have one.

Importing from your PlayStation account (optional)

The import uses a bookmark you add to your own browser and run on Sony's site, library.playstation.com, while you're signed in there. Its code is public at psn-import.js. It asks Sony, using the sign-in you already have on Sony's site, for two lists: the PS4 and PS5 games you've bought or claimed, and the PS4 and PS5 games you've played, with when you last played them. It copies those lists to your clipboard. It never sees your PlayStation password and sends nothing to us or anyone else. Sony handles those requests under its own privacy policy, just as when you use its site.

When you paste the lists into the wall, your browser matches them against the games on the site and, if you choose Import, tags the games you own and marks the games you've played. The pasted lists aren't sent anywhere. So that My library can also show the games you own that aren't part of PS Plus, a copy of their names and platforms is kept in your browser only: it isn't synced to your account or included in backups, and Forget this list on that page deletes it. The marks and tags they create are saved like any others: in your browser, and in encrypted form in your account if you have one.

Copying a prompt for an AI assistant

The Copy prompt button puts a request, including the ratings and notes it draws on, on your device's clipboard. Nothing is sent anywhere by the site. If you paste it into another service, that service's own privacy policy applies.

Service providers

Two companies host parts of the site. They handle network information, such as your IP address, as any website host does when your device connects to it.

  • GitHub (GitHub Pages) serves the old address, which forwards visitors to wallofpsplus.com. GitHub logs visitors' IP addresses for security purposes, as described in the GitHub General Privacy Statement.
  • Cloudflare (Workers, D1 and Web Analytics) serves the site at wallofpsplus.com, runs the account service and its database, and provides the visit counts described above. Cloudflare processes request information to deliver and protect the service, as described in the Cloudflare Privacy Policy. Cloudflare's servers may be outside Canada, including in the United States, where local laws may allow authorities to access data. The account data stored there is encrypted and doesn't name you.

We don't sell, rent or share information with anyone else, and we don't use it for advertising. Cloudflare says it doesn't fingerprint visitors or use Web Analytics data for advertising.

Links on the site to the PlayStation Store, Metacritic, HowLongToBeat, Wikipedia and YouTube take you to other websites with their own privacy policies.

How long information is kept

Information in your browser stays until you clear it, or use "Sign out and clear this browser". Account data stays until you delete the account. When you press Delete my account, the account's record is removed from the database straight away. Cloudflare keeps short-term recovery copies of the database for a limited period (currently up to 30 days), after which deleted data is gone for good. We may also delete accounts if the site is shut down.

Security

Passwords are stretched with PBKDF2 (310,000 rounds of SHA-256) in your browser, records are encrypted with AES-GCM before they leave it, and all connections use HTTPS. Repeated wrong passwords lock an account for a while. No system is perfectly secure, so please use a password you don't use anywhere else, and download a backup now and then from the Your data section.

Your choices and rights

  • See your data: everything the site holds about you is visible on the site. Your data → Download backup gives you a complete copy.
  • Correct it: change any mark, rating, note or added game on the site.
  • Delete it: use Account → Delete my account, and clear your browser's site data. To withdraw only your community votes, untick Add my ratings to the community scores.
  • Take it elsewhere: the backup file is plain JSON.

Because the account service only holds an id derived from your username and data it can't decrypt, we can't find or identify your account from anything other than your username and password. If you have a question or concern about your privacy, contact us through the GitHub issues page and we'll respond as best we can. Canadian residents may also contact the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada.

Children

The site isn't directed at children under 13. If you're under the age of majority where you live, please check with a parent or guardian before creating an account.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, we'll post the new version here with a new effective date. If a change affects how account data is handled, we'll also note it on the site.
